How Much Should a Small Business Spend on Meta Ads in India? (2026 Guide)

It's the first question every business owner asks us, and the most honest answer is: it depends on what a customer is worth to you. But "it depends" is a useless answer, so this guide gives you real numbers — the budget ranges we see working for Indian small businesses right now, and what each level actually buys you.

The short answer

A ₹30,000–₹50,000 monthly media budget is one possible starting scenario, not a minimum that guarantees results. A smaller budget may be viable when acquisition costs are low; a high-cost service may need more. Decide how much you can afford to test using your margins, target cost per customer and available cash. Management fees, creative production and website work are separate.

What each budget level buys you

₹20,000–₹30,000/month — Testing budget

Illustrative use: test one offer with a small set of creatives. At an assumed ₹750 acquisition cost, ₹30,000 would buy about 40 customers in a month. That is a planning calculation, not a prediction. Actual conversion volume determines how much you can learn.

₹50,000–₹1,00,000/month — Growth budget

Illustrative use: give promising creative tests more room and compare results over a defined period. At an assumed ₹750 acquisition cost, ₹75,000 would buy 100 customers. Keep prospecting and retargeting budgets tied to audience size and incremental results rather than automatically creating more campaigns.

₹1,00,000–₹5,00,000/month — Scale budget

Consider this level only after measuring acquisition costs, contribution margin and cash-flow needs. More spend does not guarantee proportionally more orders. Increase gradually and check whether the next tranche of spend produces profitable additional customers.

The formula: work backwards from your numbers

Forget rules of thumb. Do this maths:

  1. Customer contribution: Estimate revenue after product, fulfilment, returns and other variable costs. Count repeat purchases only when you have evidence for them.
  2. Acceptable acquisition cost: If an order brings in ₹3,000, first calculate its contribution margin. Use ₹750 as an acquisition target only if it leaves enough to cover fees, overheads and your required profit.
  3. Monthly target: 100 customers × ₹750 = ₹75,000/month ad budget.

If the maths doesn't work at your current margins, the answer isn't a bigger budget — it's a better offer, better creative, or a better funnel. That's exactly what we check in a free audit.

Three mistakes that burn small budgets

  • Splitting ₹30,000 across 6 campaigns. Each campaign gets ₹5,000 and none of them learns anything. Run fewer campaigns with meaningful budgets.
  • Optimising for the wrong event. If you sell online, optimise for purchases — not traffic, not engagement. Cheap clicks that don't buy are expensive.
  • Judging a test without enough evidence. Set a loss limit and a review window in advance. Consider conversion volume and the purchase cycle before deciding. Learning status depends on the optimisation event and account setup; no rupee threshold guarantees that learning will end.

Should you hire an agency at a small budget?

Compare the agency fee, media spend and expected contribution from additional customers together. A management fee can consume much of a small test budget, so self-management or a focused consultation may fit better. Ask for a relevant case study with dates, spend, attribution assumptions and the changes made. An agency should explain its approach without promising a particular ROAS or payback period.

The bottom line

Choose a test budget you can afford to lose, work backwards from an evidence-based acquisition target, and track completed sales and contribution margin. The ranges above are planning examples for Indian businesses, not account benchmarks or a guarantee. Scale when the measured economics support it.
