Google Ads vs Meta Ads for Service Businesses

Key takeaways

  • Compare the cost of qualified opportunities and customers as well as the cost of forms
  • Choose a first test that matches buying intent, available creative and your sales process
  • Keep advertising spend, management fees and service delivery costs visible in the decision

Google Ads or Meta Ads is a useful question only after you define the job you want to win. An emergency repair, a planned renovation and a recurring business service involve different decisions. The channel should fit that decision, the value of the work and your ability to follow up. A cheap enquiry that your team cannot serve is an expense, however attractive its dashboard looks.

This comparison focuses on Google Search campaigns and Meta lead generation. Google Ads includes other campaign types, so a proposal for Search should not be treated as interchangeable with every Google campaign.

Start with the customer decision

Google describes Search campaigns as a way to reach people searching for relevant products or services. That makes Search a reasonable starting hypothesis when customers already know the service they need. It does not establish that local demand is large enough, clicks are affordable or the people searching are a good fit.

For Meta, think about what would make someone pause, understand the offer and request a conversation. A clear demonstration, a specific problem or credible project evidence may give you something useful to test. An unfamiliar service usually needs more explanation than a familiar urgent repair.

These are planning prompts, not a prediction that either platform will produce a particular type or volume of lead. Evaluate the actual enquiries your campaign produces.

Choose the enquiry path deliberately

Meta supports instant forms and forms on your website. An instant form can reduce the steps required to express interest. A website can provide more context, explain scope and support a booking or quotation journey. The right choice depends on what someone needs to understand before contacting you.

For a straightforward service, a concise form may be sufficient. For a project requiring measurements, several stakeholders or a substantial commitment, explain the assessment process first. Ask for information your team actually uses. Service type, location and intended timing are more useful than extra fields added only to make a form look serious.

Set the test around job economics

A job's invoice value is not its advertising allowance. Deduct the labour, materials, travel and other direct costs needed to deliver it. Then decide what must remain for overhead, risk and profit before estimating an acquisition allowance.

Hypothetical example, not a benchmark: a job brings in 3,000 currency units and requires 1,800 in direct delivery costs. That leaves 1,200 before acquisition and overhead. If the owner reserves 800 for overhead and desired profit, the remaining 400 is a planning ceiling for acquiring that customer. Different delivery costs or profit requirements change the ceiling.

Advertising spend is only one acquisition expense. Include agreed management fees, creative costs and any relevant sales costs when evaluating the overall commitment. Do not compare a platform's advertising-only cost per customer with a ceiling intended to cover all acquisition work.

Run a test you can interpret

  1. Select one service and a real service area — describe the work you can accept and the locations you can cover economically.
  2. Define a qualified enquiry — agree the required service fit, location and purchasing circumstances before reviewing results.
  3. Prepare the response process — name the person who handles leads and establish coverage for the times you invite enquiries.
  4. Set a spending limit and review point — base them on available cash, likely sales-cycle length and how much uncertainty you can tolerate.
  5. Record outcomes — connect enquiries to qualified conversations, quotes, bookings and completed work.

If your resources are limited, one well-instrumented test may be easier to interpret than several underfunded tests. If both channels run, keep the offer and qualification rules clear enough to understand why results differ. Use the qualified lead and acquisition cost guide to build the scorecard.

Adapt the offer to the market

A service business in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia or India should plan around its own trading area, customer language, local currency and appointment coverage. Country-wide targeting makes little commercial sense if the team can serve only a few neighbourhoods. A cross-border consultancy has a different reach, but still needs realistic time-zone coverage and a clear delivery model.

As a hypothetical planning choice, an urgent repair business might first investigate local Search demand. A business selling a planned outdoor transformation might test visual explanations on Meta. Neither example establishes which channel will win for your business.

Make the next decision from sales evidence

Compare qualified conversations and acquired customers after allowing the relevant enquiries time to progress. Review rejection reasons as well as averages. Low quality may point to an unclear offer; good enquiries with few bookings may point to follow-up, pricing or capacity. The diagnosis determines the next change.

Bring your service list, job economics and recent lead outcomes to a service business marketing review. A useful channel recommendation should explain the test, its total cost and what evidence will justify continuing it.

Frequently asked questions

Which platform is cheaper for a service business?

There is no dependable universal winner. Compare the cost of qualified enquiries and acquired customers for your offer and market. Include management, creative and relevant sales costs when deciding whether the overall acquisition cost is affordable.

Should I run Google Ads and Meta Ads together?

That can be reasonable when each channel has a clear role and you can fund, measure and follow up both. Starting with one focused test may be more useful when budget, creative capacity or reliable outcome data is limited.

Does a lower cost per lead mean a better campaign?

Only if the leads retain sufficient quality and turn into valuable customers. A cheaper form submission can still produce a higher acquisition cost when few enquiries qualify or buy.
