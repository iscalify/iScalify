Google Ads for Australian Trades With Dispatch in Mind

By the Iscalify teamUpdated 26 November 20266 min read

A practical guide to connecting an Australian trade company’s advertising with crew availability, local service coverage, suitable job requests and confirmed work

Key takeaways

  • Set campaign coverage and promises around actual dispatch capacity
  • Check account time zones before relying on ad schedules
  • Use job outcomes and enquiry reasons to guide account changes

For a trade business, an advertising enquiry is the start of a scheduling and scoping process. A suitable request can still become a poor use of time if it requires an impractical journey, arrives outside staffed hours or describes work the team cannot accept. Google Ads planning should therefore include the person who manages the diary, not just the person managing the account.

For an Australian business, make service postcodes, customer time zones and realistic appointment windows explicit. The following framework connects campaign decisions with the work the team can deliver.

Start with the diary and the service area

Identify the work you want to book over the next planning period. Note the available crew, the jobs they can handle and any equipment or site information required. Then map the postcodes and suburbs that fit those arrangements. Advertising more widely is a business decision about delivery capacity, not simply a way to increase reach.

Keep a separate list of conditional areas. A longer trip may make sense for a larger project but not a small call-out. Explain any such distinction in the enquiry process. Ask for the job address early enough to avoid discussing an appointment the team cannot provide.

Review coverage against actual travel and booking records. Neighbouring suburbs do not necessarily create equally practical routes for your particular business.

Check which clock the account uses

Google's ad scheduling instructions state that schedules use the account's time zone. Confirm how that corresponds to the local time of the customers you serve before relying on an opening-hours schedule.

This matters when one person manages campaigns for several operating regions. Document the intended customer hours beside the account settings, and review the mapping when relevant local clock changes occur. Keep the team's actual availability in the same planning record.

Do not automatically stop all advertising when the phone desk closes. A customer may be happy to request an estimate for a later reply. The decision depends on the contact route and promise: a staffed call, an enquiry form or a clearly described callback request. Avoid implying immediate attendance unless the business can deliver it.

Group work by the decision it requires

Different jobs may need different information before an estimate can be prepared. A small maintenance task might be assessed from a description and photos. A larger project could require measurements or a site visit. Explain that distinction on the page and in the first response.

Use campaign or ad group separation when it helps align the search, message, page and measurement. If two job types compete for the same limited crew but have very different scope, review them separately in the business report even if the account structure remains simple.

Keep advertised work within the company's real capabilities. Mention credentials, warranties or specialist experience only when the owner can verify the exact claim.

Use searches to investigate mismatches

Review searches alongside the enquiries they generated. Google's search terms report helps show what triggered ads and identify irrelevant searches. Investigate whether customers are seeking a service, materials, training or employment.

An exclusion should solve a documented mismatch. For example, a business offering painting services might find enquiries for paint supplies irrelevant, while a customer asking about preparation costs could be a suitable project lead. Check the intended reach of a negative keyword before using it across unrelated campaigns.

When an apparently relevant search produces unsuitable enquiries, review the page as well. The missing information may be the service boundary, accepted job type or estimate process.

Build the page around a real next step

Give the customer enough information to make a sensible request. State the work offered, coverage, how quotes are prepared and what happens after contact. A form can ask for the service postcode, a short description and a suitable contact time. Invite photos when they help assessment, with a clear explanation of why they are useful.

Use current project examples that show the task and completed work. A photo needs context to demonstrate relevance. If the final scope depends on inspection, say so instead of suggesting that every enquiry can receive an immediate fixed quote. Diagnose the stage that is losing work

Record the outcome of enquiries using consistent reasons. A simple review can reveal whether the constraint sits in advertising, routing, qualification or available capacity.

Requests outside coverageEnquiry addresses and campaign geographyClarify boundaries and review targeting
Missed callsActual call times and staffingAlign the schedule or offer a callback route
Unsuitable job typesSearches and page scopeRefine the service message and exclusions
Suitable jobs cannot be bookedCrew capacity and customer timingAdjust availability messaging or the work being promoted

Compare booked and completed work as well as enquiry volume. Note lead times and cancellations so that a recent campaign is not judged before its estimates have had time to reach a decision.

A hypothetical painting crew example

Imagine a painting company accepting residential projects in selected Brisbane suburbs. Its next available project window is several weeks away, but its ad implies immediate availability. Clarifying the start window and estimate process could help customers self-select before contacting the team. The useful measure would be suitable estimate requests and eventual bookings, not simply fewer enquiries. This is a hypothetical example, not an Iscalify client result.

Frequently asked questions

Should trades advertise only while the phone is staffed?

It depends on the promise and contact route. Calls that imply immediate help need suitable coverage. A clear estimate or callback form can still be useful outside those hours if the team follows up as described.

Which time zone does a Google Ads schedule use?

Google Ads schedules use the account time zone. Check its relationship to the customer’s local hours, especially when one account or team manages several regions.

Why can relevant trade searches still produce unsuitable leads?

The location, scope, timing or availability may not match what the customer needs. Review the actual enquiry and landing page before assuming the search itself should be excluded.
